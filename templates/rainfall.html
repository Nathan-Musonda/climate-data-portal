Is rainfall increasing or decreasing over time?
This chart shows whether rainfall has been going up or down across the years, which is important for water and food security planning.
What to look for: A downward trend means the area is getting drier over time, which is a warning sign for rain-fed agriculture and water supply. An upward trend means wetter conditions, which can increase flood risk. A flat trend means rainfall has stayed relatively consistent.
How much rain fell each year?
Compares total rainfall for each year between 2015 and 2025, making it easy to spot the wettest and driest years.
What to look for: Short bars mean that year was dry. Tall bars mean above-average rainfall and possible flood risk. Years that stand out at either extreme are important for understanding Zambia's climate variability.
Which months get the most rain?
Shows Zambia's seasonal rainfall pattern: which months are wet and which are dry throughout the year.
Zambia's rainy season runs from November to March. This is when most of the year's rainfall occurs. The dry season from May to September sees almost no rain, so shifts in timing can directly affect planting and harvesting.
Which provinces receive the most rain?
Compares average rainfall across all provinces for the selected year, showing where water availability is highest and lowest.
What this means: Northern, Luapula, and Copperbelt provinces typically receive the most rainfall. Southern and Western provinces receive the least, making them more vulnerable to drought and more dependent on water storage, irrigation, and drought-resistant crops.
How is rainfall spread across the year?
Shows whether most rainfall readings were low, moderate, or high, giving a picture of how consistent or variable rainfall is.
What to look for: If most readings cluster at the low end, the majority of days had little or no rain, which is normal for Zambia because rain falls in a short season. A wide spread means rainfall was highly variable, with very wet days mixed among many dry days. Very high single-day readings are the ones most likely to cause localised flooding.
What does Zambia's rainfall mean for everyday life?

More than 80% of Zambia's farming is rain-fed, meaning crops depend entirely on seasonal rainfall to grow. When the rainy season arrives late, ends early, or delivers less rain than usual, the impact is felt immediately through smaller harvests, higher food prices, and reduced water availability for drinking and livestock. Provinces in the south and west consistently receive less rain than the national average, making them the most vulnerable to drought.

About this data

Rainfall records cover all of Zambia's provinces from 2015 to 2025, sourced from the Zambia Meteorological Department (ZMD), the SADC Climate Services Centre, and NASA Earth Observations. All values are in millimetres (mm), the standard unit for measuring rainfall.
