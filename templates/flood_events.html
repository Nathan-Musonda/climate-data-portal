Rainfall over time
How average rainfall changes across the selected reporting period.
What to look for: A rising trend means rainfall is increasing, which can raise flood risk. A falling trend points to drier conditions and may shift attention toward drought.
How wet was it compared to normal?
SPI above 0 means wetter than normal; above 1 is significant wetness; above 2 is extreme wetness.
What to look for: Values above 0 show wetter-than-normal conditions. Values above +1 are warning signs, while values above +2 indicate extreme wetness.
Ground water level over time
Ground saturation context that can turn rainfall into stronger surface flooding.
What this means: Think of the ground like a sponge. When it is already saturated, extra rainfall cannot soak in easily and can flow into rivers, roads, farms, and settlements.
Which provinces received the most rain?
Compares provinces with the highest average rainfall in the active view.
What this means: Provinces with consistently high rainfall carry greater flood exposure, especially when high rainfall aligns with wet soil and rainy-season months.

What does this mean for Zambia?

Flooding in Zambia is closely linked to heavy rainfall during the rainy season, especially when the ground is already saturated. This page helps identify the years, months, and provinces where rainfall pressure is strongest so planning, early warning, farming decisions, and infrastructure work can be better targeted.

About this data

This page uses climate records in the portal to identify province-level flood-risk signals from rainfall, SPI wetness, soil moisture, and date fields.

The results are for climate analysis and planning support. For emergency flood information, use official alerts from Zambia's disaster management and meteorological authorities.
