How severe has drought been overall?
Shows the full breakdown of drought conditions — what proportion of time was drought-free, mild, moderate, severe, or extreme.
🌵 How to read this: A large green slice means conditions were mostly fine — good news for farmers and water supply. A large orange or red slice means drought was frequent and serious. For Zambia, any significant portion in the severe or extreme categories is a concern, as these conditions directly reduce crop yields and water availability for communities.
Is drought getting worse over time?
Tracks drought severity year by year — showing whether conditions are improving, staying the same, or getting worse across the selected area.
📈 What to look for: A rising line means drought is becoming more frequent or severe over time — a serious warning for agriculture and water planning. A falling line means conditions are improving. A flat line means no significant change. If the most recent years show the highest values, that is an urgent signal for early warning action.
How dry has the ground been over time?
Shows how much water was in the soil over time — drops in this chart are a direct early warning sign of drought stress on crops and land.
💧 What this means: Think of the soil like a sponge. When it dries out, crops cannot absorb enough moisture to grow — even if some rain falls later. Prolonged periods of low ground water during the growing season (November–March) lead directly to reduced harvests and food insecurity for farming communities.
Which provinces are most affected by drought?
Compares drought severity across all of Zambia's provinces for the selected year — showing where conditions are worst and where they are more manageable.
🗺️ What this means: Southern, Western, and Eastern provinces typically experience the most severe drought conditions in Zambia. These are also the provinces most dependent on rain-fed agriculture — making them the most vulnerable to food insecurity when drought strikes. Provinces with lower bars are more drought-resilient, generally because they receive higher and more consistent rainfall throughout the year.

🌵 What does drought mean for Zambia?

Drought is one of the most damaging climate hazards in Zambia. Because over 80% of farming is rain-fed, even one dry season can reduce harvests significantly — driving up food prices and pushing vulnerable households into food insecurity. Southern and Western provinces face the most frequent and severe drought conditions, but no province is immune. As temperatures rise across Zambia, drought is also becoming more heat-driven: even in years with near-normal rainfall, higher evaporation means the land dries out faster. This page helps identify which areas and years need the most urgent attention for drought preparedness, early warning, and irrigation investment.

About this data

Drought indicators cover all of Zambia's districts from 2015 to 2025, sourced from the Zambia Meteorological Department (ZMD), NASA Earth Observations, and the SADC Climate Services Centre. Drought severity is classified using internationally recognised thresholds based on ground water levels and rainfall conditions.
